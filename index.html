I previously contributed to embodied intelligence projects at Shenzhen Shita Robotics Technology Co., Ltd., focusing on visual-tactile data collection, multimodal training, and edge deployment on real-world robots. In parallel, I also maintained a visual-tactile optical-flow estimation SDK, optimized hardware solutions, and deployed algorithms.

I received my B.S. in IoT Engineering from Jiujiang University, where I spent three years as a researcher at the JJU CVI Lab. There, I worked on object detection, medical image segmentation, and edge AI—from problem formulation and experiments to model deployment.

My current research focuses on tactile perception, multimodal robot learning, and algorithm optimization. It spans visual-tactile data collection, multimodal policy training, sim-to-real transfer, and edge-device deployment, while progressively exploring world models for embodied intelligence.

Goal: Bridge perception and action through vision, touch, and learning.

Research Interest: The intersection of visuotactile robot learning, sim-to-real transfer, and edge AI.

Research Question: How can we integrate vision, touch, and learning to build reliable robot intelligence?

Robots: I work with Piper robotic arms and visuotactile sensors, and build contact-rich environments in Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab.

A fabric-classification prototype for short tactile interactions, fusing tactile pressure-image sequences with six-dimensional physical states through ResNet-18, a causal TCN, and attention pooling.
